06 / ASEMIC WRITING FROM CHAOS

Chaotic Characters

Characters from a script that never existed. Each one is drawn stroke by stroke from two copies of the simplest chaotic equation, so it looks like writing but belongs to no language and says nothing at all.

How it works

xn+1 = 4 xn(1 − xn)

u = 2⁄π arcsin √x

  1. Seed. The seed text is hashed into two starting values, x₀ and y₀, between 0 and 1. Change a single letter and both start somewhere else.
  2. Stir. Each map is iterated several times for every number it gives out. At r = 4 the logistic map doubles any error on average every step, so after a few dozen steps the starting digits are completely forgotten.
  3. Flatten. The map spends most of its time near 0 and 1. The arcsine change of variables turns its values into a uniform u, and bits from deep inside both maps are combined with XOR.
  4. Write. Each character sits on a loose grid of 3 × 4 points, nudged a little so no two are alike. The chaotic numbers choose how many strokes to make, where each starts (usually on a point an earlier stroke touched, so the pieces join), which neighbouring point to move to next, whether that move is straight or curved and how far it bends, and whether to add a dot or a small tail.

Nothing here is drawn from a real alphabet, though now and then a simple shape may resemble a letter by coincidence. The same seed always writes the same manuscript.
